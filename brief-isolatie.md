Why is air quality relevant now?

Before, people didn't think twice about letting
fresh air in. Houses contained more gaps around windows, wide
letterbox gaps in their door, and
inhabitants simply had windows and doors open more frequently.
They often had fresh air without thinking about it.

With increasing energy prices and interest in sustainability, people
improve their house and change their behavior.  Houses become better
insulated: window frames are replaced, gaps are closed. Inhabitants
more often keep their windows and ventillation grilles closed at night
to keep the warm air inside and reduce heating to save energy.
Additionally, with increasing temperatures in summer, inhabitants
install air conditioning and keep the windows and grilles closed at night to keep
the cool air inside too.

In modern buildings with central ventilation, often those built after
2004, fresh air is brought separately in each room by conduits and air
quality remains good when these changes happen. However in “older”
houses built before 2000, the existing active ventilation is often
insufficient. It may extract air only centrally and not help bringing
fresh air inside bedrooms. In some cases, the inhabitants even keep it
on a low setting or disabled because of noise and energy use by the
ventilation motor. For these houses, insulation and behavior changes
can have drastic negative effects on air quality indoors.

This happens almost by accident: by doing “good things̛” (isolation,
energy savings) the inhabitants do not realize they are making their
inside air bad.

Air quality is also invisible, and often doesn't change the smell in a
noticeable way. Symptoms of health issues may only appear slowly, and
be difficult to link to poorer air quality.

Government agencies and advisors often say that “insulation and
ventilation go hand in hand” but it's often not clear for inhabitants
what ventilation precisely entails.  Some people think that airco also
provides ventilation (it does not). Other people think that opening
windows just once or twice a day is sufficient (it is not).

This is why we are campaining to increase awareness of air quality
indoors. We would also like to provide more information on what
someone can do to improve the air quality in their living spaces.
