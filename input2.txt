Core messages:

- poorly ventilated living spaces, in particular bedrooms, can cause poorer sleep and health issues
- poor ventilation is strongly correlated with elevated CO2. By measuring CO2 we can estimate
  ventilation effectiveness.
- multiple organizations and government are building up norms and guidelines for ventilation to keep
  living spaces <1000ppm, even though the Netherlands is lagging behind currently.


Relevance:
- not a new problem: do you remember stuffed classrooms in winter when you were young?
- until recently not as much a problem in homes because of poor insulation. More a problem now we insulate better
- always a problem in rooms without windows or closed windows without ventilation gap

Ineffective:
- plants
- open windows once (morning/evening)
- AC
- air filter

Unrelated problems/solutions:
- VOCs: mostly in kitchens, can be removed with carbon activated filters. We care more about living spaces (living room, bedroom) and these filters don't apply to CO2, NOx etc.
- CO: very poisonous, but also quite rare. CO2 and other pollutants are naturally emitted by breathing. CO detectors don't detect elevated CO2.

Myth busting:
- "CO₂ is harmless—it’s what we breathe out!" -> Truth: While not toxic like CO, chronic exposure at >1,000 ppm impairs cognition and can cause health issues
- "I don’t feel anything, so it’s fine." -> Truth: Symptoms are subtle (e.g., brain fog feels like "normal tiredness"). Many people adapt without realizing they’re impaired.
- "My house is old and drafty, so I’m safe." -> Truth: Drafts ≠ ventilation. Old homes can have pockets of high CO₂ (e.g., a sealed bedroom with closed doors).
- "Plants help a little." -> Truth: A 10m² room would need ~100 large plants to match the CO₂ removal of one open window. Effectively zero impact.
- "Air purifiers with CO₂ sensors are enough." -> Truth: Most consumer air purifiers do NOT remove CO₂ (they lack gas-phase filters for CO₂). Only dedicated CO₂ scrubbers (expensive, industrial) work.
