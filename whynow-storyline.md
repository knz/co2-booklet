# Storyline proposal: "Why should you care now?" (A5 sheet)

Status: approved by the user on 2026-09-21 and built as `whynow/`. The
text on the sheet was shortened to fit A5; §7 lists how the sheet differs
from the draft text below. The text files in `whynow/` are authoritative
for the wording, this file for the reasoning and the wording limits.
Based on `brief-isolatie.md` and the source check of 2026-09-21 (findings
in `sources/archive/_report-why-now-local.md` and `_report-why-now-web.md`,
summary in `changelog/20260921-why-care-now-page.md`).

Purpose (user, 2026-09-21): more "political" than the existing infographic;
meant to stimulate discussion more than only to inform. Sender of the "we":
members of the city council of Diemen. The content rules still apply:
wording stays within what the sources support.

Draft text below is English working copy, about 190 words without the
footer; it will need trimming to fit A5 once the Dutch version exists.
Dutch headlines are suggestions. Source names in square brackets; entries
marked (new) are not yet in `sources/references.yml`. Figures marked
(agent-reported) come from the source-check agents and were not re-read by
the main session.

## 1. The argument in four sentences

1. In most homes, fresh air for the bedroom has always depended on gaps,
   grilles and habits, not on a system.
2. We are now closing the gaps, heating less and keeping things shut: good
   for the energy bill, but it removes fresh air that used to come in
   without anyone arranging it.
3. Nobody notices, because stale air has no clear smell and the complaints
   are vague.
4. The standard advice ("insulate and ventilate") does not tell people what
   ventilating means in their type of home. That is the gap this campaign
   wants to discuss and close.

## 2. How this differs from the brief

The brief contrasts new homes (after 2004, air stays good) with old homes
(before 2000, at risk). The source check does not support build years as
the dividing line:

- No rule change found around 2003–2004; Bouwbesluit 2003 sets capacities,
  not a system type [Bouwbesluit 2003 afd. 3.10 (new)].
- Of homes built since 2000, about 1 in 4 has balanced ventilation; the
  rest still get bedroom air through grilles [Milieu Centraal,
  balansventilatie (new); WoON 2012, ECN/RIGO 2013 (new)].
- Balanced systems in new-build often underperformed in field studies
  [BBA 2011; RIVM Jongeneel 2011 (new)], although on bedroom CO₂ they did
  clearly better than the other systems [MONICAIR 2016 (new)].

Proposed instead: divide by **what the resident can see**: grilles (or
nothing) versus supply vents in every room. This covers most Diemen homes,
including recent ones, which seems to serve the discussion purpose at least
as well. It is a change from the brief and is the user's call.

Alternative kept open: stay closer to the brief with softened years ("many
homes built before about 2000 …"). The WoON data supports this only
loosely, since extraction-only systems were still the majority in new-build
in 2006–2008 [RIGO 2009] and, per a secondary source, in 2017.

## 3. Block by block

### Title

- **EN:** Why should you care now?
- **NL:** Waarom juist nu? (the headline `script.md` already uses for the
  booklet paragraph on this topic)
- **Subtitle EN:** We make our homes warmer and cheaper to heat. The fresh
  air needs attention too.

### Block 1 — Fresh air used to come in by itself

NL: *Frisse lucht kwam vroeger vanzelf binnen*

> Older homes let air in through gaps around windows and doors, and
> through the letterbox. It cost heat, but it also brought in some fresh
> air, and nobody had to think about it.

Sources: GGD-richtlijn, Duijm 2009 p.19 (new); TNO 2007 p.40; Milieu
Centraal, ventilatie in huis: "via naden en kieren" (new).

Wording limits: "some fresh air", not "enough". TNO's 2004–05 measurements
found older homes also often above 1,200 ppm, and the booklet's myth table
already says an old draughty home is not necessarily fine. "People had
windows open more often" is dropped: no source found.

### Block 2 — We are closing the gaps

NL: *We maken alles dicht*

> Energy is expensive, and we want to use less of it. So we insulate,
> replace window frames and seal gaps. Many people heat less, and some keep
> grilles and windows shut to keep the warmth in. More homes also have air
> conditioning, which cools the air in the room but brings in no fresh air.
> All of this helps the energy bill. But the fresh air that came in by
> accident is gone.

Sources: Milieu Centraal, natuurlijke ventilatie; CLO/CBS insulation
measures 1982–2018 (new); Milieu Centraal/Motivaction 2023 (new; heating
less, n=1,042); GGD Zaanstreek-Waterland panel 2023 (new; self-reported
ventilation); RIVM radon 2024 (calls less ventilation "aannemelijk", did
not measure it); CBS 2024 (new; airco ownership); RIVM: "een airco of
ventilator zorgt niet voor ventilatie" (new).

Wording limits: "some keep grilles and windows shut" rests on self-reports.
"At night" is dropped. The airco sentence states what the device does, not
a trend of closed windows: CBS reports 72% open windows in the evening or
at night to cool.

### Block 3 — Which home do you have?

NL: *Welk huis heb jij?* Two columns, each with an icon.

**A. Grilles above the windows, or no grilles at all**

> Most homes, including many built after 2000. Fresh air for the bedroom
> comes only through the grille or the window. A fan in the kitchen or
> bathroom only takes air out. Grille shut? Then hardly any fresh air
> reaches the bedroom. Many people also keep the fan on its lowest setting
> because of the noise.

**B. Supply vents in every room (balanced ventilation, "WTW")**

> About 1 in 4 homes built since 2000. The system brings fresh air to every
> room, also with the windows shut. Usually better, if it is well installed
> and not left on the lowest setting.

Sources: Milieu Centraal, ventilatie in huis / mechanische ventilatie /
balansventilatie (new; 1975, "1 op de 4", grilles must stay open); RVO
Infoblad 2014 (new); TNO 2019 (new; sheltered-side bedrooms); Bader 2009,
RIGO 2009, BBA 2011 (lowest setting, noise); MONICAIR 2016 (new; bedroom
CO₂ per system type, 62 homes, consortium includes manufacturers).

Wording limits: no build-year boundary. "Off" and "energy use of the fan"
are dropped (only "lowest setting because of noise" is supported). Column B
says "usually better", not "stays good". Candidate extra fact for column A,
if space allows: TNO 2007 found 84% of 163 extraction systems below the
required flow at their highest setting (agent-reported).

### Block 4 — Doing the right thing can make the air worse

NL: *Goed bezig, en toch slechtere lucht*

> Insulating and saving energy are good things to do. But in a home of type
> A they can make the indoor air noticeably worse, without anyone meaning
> to.

Sources: RIVM 2011, gezondheid binnenmilieu energiebesparing (new);
Signalen Leefomgeving 2024; Milieu Centraal; Fisk, Singer & Chan 2020
review (new); Földváry 2017 (new).

Wording limits: "can", and no "drastic". No claim of a measured national
trend: TNO 2007 found no simple build-year pattern, MONICAIR found leakier
homes did not have better CO₂, and the Fisk review calls the CO₂ data
"very limited" while damp and mould mostly decreased after retrofits.

Optional number, to discuss: in 20 Slovak flats measured before and after
renovation, median night-time CO₂ went from 1,300 to 1,870 ppm [Földváry
2017] (agent-reported). It is concrete, but small and foreign; my lean is
to leave it off the sheet and keep it for the source list.

### Block 5 — You don't notice it

NL: *Je merkt het niet*

> CO₂ has no smell, and you get used to stale air within minutes. The
> complaints that go with stale air are vague and hard to link to the air.
> A CO₂ meter shows what you cannot sense.

Sources: Gunnarsen & Fanger 1992; Zhang 2017; GGD-richtlijn, Duijm 2009
§3.6.1 "De klachten zijn aspecifiek" (new).

Wording limits: "symptoms appear slowly" is dropped (Longfonds describes
complaints as mostly short-lived). If complaints are named, use the same
ones and the same hedging as booklet p2–p3.

### Block 6 — "Insulate and ventilate". But what does that mean?

NL: *"Isoleren en ventileren": maar wat is ventileren?*

> The advice is that insulation and ventilation go hand in hand. What
> often stays unsaid:
>
> - Ventilating means fresh outdoor air coming in all day and night.
>   Airing a room for a short while is not the same.
> - An air conditioner does not ventilate.
> - An extractor fan only works if air can come in somewhere: keep the
>   grilles open.

Sources: GGD Leefomgeving 2020, "Isolatie en ventilatie gaan hand in hand"
(new; the only place the literal phrase was found); Milieu Centraal, woning
ventileren; GGD-richtlijn p.60 "Luchten geeft slechts een kortdurende
verbetering" (new); RIVM (airco); Milieu Centraal, mechanische ventilatie.

Wording limits: the three lines are corrections, not claims about how many
people believe otherwise. The "67% think a quarter of an hour a day is
enough" figure is not used: found only through a regional news item, the
original Milieu Centraal survey was not located. No evidence found that
people believe an airco ventilates. "It is often unclear to residents" is
softened to "what often stays unsaid", which the sheet itself demonstrates.

### Block 7 — Closing, in the first person

NL: *Daarom vragen wij hier aandacht voor*

> That is why we, members of the city council of Diemen, want more
> attention for the air in our homes: clear information about what
> ventilating means in your kind of home, and CO₂ meters that residents
> can borrow. What can you do yourself? Read the guide.

To check by the user: that the two asks match the motion as submitted.

## 5. Dropped from the brief, and why

| Brief | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "built after 2004" / "before 2000" | replaced by system type | contradicted, see §2 |
| modern homes: "air quality remains good" | "usually better, if …" | BBA 2011, MONICAIR |

| "symptoms appear slowly" | "vague and hard to link" | Longfonds; GGD-richtlijn |
| "some people think airco ventilates / airing twice is enough" | stated as corrections | no usable evidence of the belief |

## 6. Open points for discussion

- System-type framing (§2) or softened build years.
- How pointed the sheet should be. Possible discussion hooks that are not
  in the brief and are **unverified**: whether insulation subsidies and
  energy advice include a ventilation check (the ISDE ventilation subsidy
  requires an insulation measure [F15]; whether the reverse holds was not
  checked); what housing associations do about ventilation when they
  renovate. These would need their own source check before use.
- Whether the Földváry number goes on the sheet (block 4).
- Citations on the sheet or only in the site's source list.
- Length: seven blocks is a lot for A5. Candidates to merge: blocks 4 and
  5, or blocks 2 and 4.

## 7. As built (2026-09-21)

Decisions by the user: system-type framing; no citations on the sheet
(sources in the site's source list); placeholders W1–W4 for the visuals.

Differences from the draft text in §3, all made to fit one A5 side in
Dutch, which runs longer than English:

- Subtitle shortened to "Our homes get warmer and cheaper to heat. And the
  fresh air?"
- Block 2: the air conditioning sentence moved to block 6; "to keep the
  warmth in" cut.
- Block 4 heading: "Good intentions, worse air" / "Toch slechtere lucht";
  "a home of type A" became "a home of the first kind", since the cards
  carry no letters.
- Block 5: "the complaints that go with stale air" became "the
  complaints".
- Block 6: the heading now carries the "hand in hand" quote; the intro is
  one line; the third point (extractor fan, keep the grilles open) was cut
  because card A of block 3 already says it.
- Air conditioning: no ownership figure. CBS 2024 gives about 12% of
  households nationally (read off a chart), but 7% in very urban
  municipalities and under 5% in rental homes, so a national figure would
  overstate it for Diemen. The sheet only says what the device does.
- Closing: "want more attention for the air in our homes" cut; the two
  asks remain. Still to check by the user against the motion.
- The Földváry number is not on the sheet; the study is in the source list.

Visuals W3 and W4 (user, 2026-09-21): the infographic's icons I3 (grille)
and I10 (heat-recovery unit) are reused as they are, at 9 mm, so the two
sheets share one icon set. Known trade-offs: on the infographic I3 stands
for an action ("open the grille") and here for a home type; I10 shows the
unit, which many residents never see, rather than the supply vents in each
room that card B describes. Alternatives considered: a matched pair of
"room corner" icons (window with grille strip / ceiling vent over a bed),
and a two-room cross-section across both cards (needs about 15–20 mm of
height, so text would have to go).

Visuals W1 and W2 (user, 2026-09-21): a flat SVG pair in the icon style,
final assets with no generator pass (`visuals/w1-gaps.svg`,
`visuals/w2-sealed.svg`). The same door (with letterbox) and window, seen
from inside. W1: three air arrows coming in through the door gap, the
letterbox and the window frame, and one thinner yellow heat arrow going
out; the user asked for fresh air to be the focus and heat secondary. Kept
to three arrows because block 1 says "some fresh air". W2: draught strips
around door and window, a brush in the letterbox, double glazing, no air
arrows, and the heat arrow turning back into the room. Revised the
same day (user): W2 repeats the three air arrows faded (grey, dashed)
with one red ✗ disc, as on the infographic, placed on the air rather
than on the door or window. No closed grille in
W2 (user had no preference; left out to keep the strip uncluttered).

Not decided: how pointed the sheet should be (§6, the two unverified
discussion hooks).
